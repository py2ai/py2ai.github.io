ModelScope's ms-swift has a scope that makes most fine-tuning frameworks look modest: supervised tuning, preference optimization, GRPO-style reinforcement learning, knowledge distillation, multimodal training, Megatron-backed large runs, quantized export, and deployment, all in one package. The current development line, version 4.7.0.dev0 under an Apache-2.0 license, organizes that breadth with an unusual pattern - rather than one monolithic trainer, a factory composes small mixin classes into exactly the trainer a task needs, and the same composition style repeats in the adapter system, the template engine, and the inference layer. The result is a codebase where the fastest way to understand any feature is to find its mixin.

It is also one of the few frameworks where the ModelScale ecosystem assumptions pay off visibly: datasets load straight from the Hub, templates read metadata registered alongside the models, and the CLI treats training, rollout sampling, evaluation, and deployment as sibling subcommands of one binary. For anyone who has outgrown single-purpose scripts, this is a case study in layering without sprawl. As always in this series, what follows is an educational tour of published source code.

Reading the overview from left to right:

- [swift/cli/main.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/cli/main.py) is the 106-line router: parse_yaml_args at line 38 merges YAML config files with command-line flags, and cli_main at line 86 maps subcommands to entry modules.
- [swift/arguments/sft_args.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/arguments/sft_args.py) holds the 434-line SFT argument tree; the RLHF counterpart in [swift/arguments/rlhf_args.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/arguments/rlhf_args.py) runs 773 lines.
- [swift/trainers/trainer_factory.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/trainers/trainer_factory.py) defines TrainerFactory at line 12, the class-composition switch for every training stage.
- [swift/trainers/mixin.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/trainers/mixin.py) is the shared behavior core, SwiftMixin at line 70 of 1495 lines with DataLoaderMixin at line 1352.
- [swift/rlhf_trainers/grpo_trainer.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/rlhf_trainers/grpo_trainer.py) is the largest single file in the package: GRPOTrainer at line 84 of 2623 lines.
- [swift/template/base.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/template/base.py) defines Template at line 43, the 2502-line encoder that turns conversations into tensors.
- [swift/tuners/base.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/tuners/base.py) exposes SwiftModel at line 31 and the Swift facade at line 702 for ten adapter types.
- [swift/infer_engine/vllm_engine.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/infer_engine/vllm_engine.py) wraps vLLM in VllmEngine at line 121 of 968 lines.

## Why You Need This

The mixin-composition pattern is the main draw. Instead of inheriting a fixed hierarchy, TrainerFactory at line 12 of [trainer_factory.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/trainers/trainer_factory.py) picks a TRL or HF trainer class, mixes in SwiftMixin for dataset handling, adapter mounting, and loss scaling, and hands back a trainer whose behavior is assembled from parts. If you have ever wished the Hugging Face Trainer were easier to customize, or wondered how [TRL](/trl/)-style trainers get extended without forking them, this file is a clean answer - and it contrasts nicely with the config-driven dispatch of [LLaMA-Factory](/llama-factory/) and the patch-first strategy of [unsloth](/unsloth/).

The template engine is the second reason. Multimodal chat models need text, images, videos, and audio interleaved into one token sequence with loss masks, and ms-swift's Template class at line 43 of [template/base.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/template/base.py) does that encoding generically - 2502 lines covering grounding boxes, tool calls, thinking modes, and per-model quirks through a metadata system. Combined with the dataset layer's media handling, it is one of the most complete public implementations of what "apply_chat_template" actually has to do for real model families. The RLHF stack rides on it: GRPO with async vLLM rollouts, DPO and its CPO/ORPO/KTO relatives, PPO, and Generative Knowledge Distillation all share the same data plumbing, an approach you can compare directly with [OpenRLHF](/openrlhf/)'s single-controller design or [axolotl](/axolotl/)'s GRPO trainers.

Finally, the adapter zoo is worth the visit on its own. SwiftModel at line 31 of [tuners/base.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/tuners/base.py) wraps any model so that LoRA, LLaMA-Pro's layer expansion, Restuning's bottleneck adapters, ReFT representation edits, NEFTune noise, and more attach through one interface - a broader catalog than [peft](/peft/) alone ships, built on top of it where possible. The Megatron subpackage extends the same argument surface to very large models the way [deepspeed](/deepspeed/) or [colossalai](/colossalai/) users would expect, and the inference engines - vLLM, LMDeploy, SGLang, plain PyTorch - all speak one protocol module, which makes the train-to-deploy loop of [lm-evaluation-harness](/lm-evaluation-harness/)-style benchmarking trivial to attach.

A run begins in [cli/main.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/cli/main.py), where cli_main at line 86 resolves the subcommand - the individual entries are tiny, with [cli/sft.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/cli/sft.py) at 20 lines and [cli/rlhf.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/cli/rlhf.py) at 7 - and torchrun relaunching is handled by use_torchrun at line 30. Arguments layer into families: SFTArgs in [arguments/sft_args.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/arguments/sft_args.py) embeds the tuner arguments of [arguments/tuner_args.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/arguments/tuner_args.py), while the RLHF tree in [arguments/rlhf_args.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/arguments/rlhf_args.py) adds rollout, reward, and vLLM server sections. Everything lands in TrainerFactory at line 12 of [trainers/trainer_factory.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/trainers/trainer_factory.py), a 75-line file that decides which composed trainer class to build from the stage, train type, and backend flags.

The mixin is where the real work hides. SwiftMixin at line 70 of [trainers/mixin.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/trainers/mixin.py) owns the shared lifecycle - loading model and template, mounting adapters, preparing losses - while DataLoaderMixin at line 1352 builds the batch streams, including the packing path from [dataset/packing.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/dataset/packing.py). Trainer construction goes through [trainers/trainer.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/trainers/trainer.py), whose 74 lines define the SFT and CPT trainer classes on top of the mixin, and upstream Trainer behavior is adjusted by [trainers/patcher.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/trainers/patcher.py) plus the helpers in [trainers/utils.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/trainers/utils.py). Dataset loading flows through [dataset/loader.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/dataset/loader.py), a 421-line loader that mixes registered datasets with per-dataset sampling weights, backed by the registry in dataset/register.py and metadata in dataset_meta.py. The terse dataset strings the CLI accepts are parsed by DatasetSyntax at line 56 of [dataset/dataset_syntax.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/dataset/dataset_syntax.py), whose docstring spells out the grammar - hf/ms::dataset_id or path:subset1/subset2#sample_count - so the #500 suffix in the quickstart below literally means "take 500 rows of this corpus". The registry side is two functions: register_dataset at line 24 of [dataset/register.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/dataset/register.py) files DatasetMeta records declared in [dataset/dataset_meta.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/dataset/dataset_meta.py) (DatasetMeta at line 174, SubsetDataset at line 27), and get_dataset_list at line 14 resolves names to metadata - the same three-part registry-plus-syntax pattern that templates and models use.

The RLHF family shows the same composition at its most elaborate. GRPOTrainer at line 84 of [rlhf_trainers/grpo_trainer.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/rlhf_trainers/grpo_trainer.py) - 2623 lines, the package's biggest file - composes RolloutTrainerMixin, SwiftMixin, and TRL's GRPO trainer. RolloutTrainerMixin at line 91 of [rlhf_trainers/rollout_mixin.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/rlhf_trainers/rollout_mixin.py) is the 1853-line bridge between training and generation: AsyncGenerateCallback at line 66 overlaps sampling with optimization, SyncRefModelCallback at line 78 keeps a remote reference model fresh, and the mixin drives either an in-process vLLM engine or the external server client at [rlhf_trainers/vllm_client.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/rlhf_trainers/vllm_client.py), 550 lines that manage weight sync and cache reset over HTTP. The preference branch centers on [rlhf_trainers/dpo_trainer.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/rlhf_trainers/dpo_trainer.py) at 519 lines (with CPO and ORPO variants nearby), PPO at [rlhf_trainers/ppo_trainer.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/rlhf_trainers/ppo_trainer.py), KTO at 130 lines, reward-model training in reward_trainer.py, and the distillation pair of gkd_trainer.py and gkd_loss.py - Generative Knowledge Distillation where the student learns from the teacher's own generations.

Templates deserve their own paragraph because they carry the multimodal weight. TemplateMeta in [template/template_meta.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/template/template_meta.py) declares a model family's system prompt, stop words, and special tokens; register_template at line 16 of [template/register.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/template/register.py) files hundreds of these registrations, and get_template at line 55 resolves one from model metadata read out of the checkpoint directory. The base Template at line 43 of [template/base.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/template/base.py) then encodes messages - including vision and audio inputs through [template/vision_utils.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/template/vision_utils.py) - into input ids, labels, and loss masks that the trainers consume directly. The adapter side mirrors this registry style: SwiftModel at line 31 and Swift at line 702 of [tuners/base.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/tuners/base.py) activate LoRA from [tuners/lora.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/tuners/lora.py) with its custom [tuners/lora_layers.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/tuners/lora_layers.py), LLaMA-Pro block expansion from [tuners/llamapro.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/tuners/llamapro.py), and the rest from sibling modules.

Serving and the model registry close the loop. InferEngine at line 22 of [infer_engine/infer_engine.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/infer_engine/infer_engine.py) defines the common request surface; VllmEngine at line 121 of [infer_engine/vllm_engine.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/infer_engine/vllm_engine.py) implements it for vLLM (with compatibility patches like _patch_vllm_dp_coordinator_timeout at line 54), while transformers_engine.py, lmdeploy_engine.py, and sglang_engine.py cover the other backends, all speaking the request shapes in [infer_engine/protocol.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/infer_engine/protocol.py). Under everything sits the model registry: model_arch.py at 926 lines maps architectures to module names, register.py at 689 lines files per-model metadata and loading adjustments, and patcher.py at 726 lines carries the per-family fixes. Model names themselves live as enum members in [model/constant.py](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift/blob/main/swift/model/constant.py), 313 lines where LLMModelType at line 6, MLLMModelType at line 144, and reward-model and BERT entries at lines 130 and 136 merge into a ModelType union at line 295 - the same three-part pattern the template and dataset systems use, which is the codebase's real signature.

## From Install to a Fine-Tuned Model

The README's 13GB one-GPU run:

```sh
pip install ms-swift
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 swift sft \
    --model Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 \
    --tuner_type lora \
    --dataset AI-ModelScope/alpaca-gpt4-data-zh#500 \
              AI-ModelScope/alpaca-gpt4-data-en#500 \
    --lora_rank 8 --lora_alpha 32 \
    --target_modules all-linear \
    --max_length 2048 --output_dir output
```

Inference and export use the same style:

```sh
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 swift infer --adapters output/checkpoint-xx --infer_backend vllm
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0 swift export --adapters output/checkpoint-xx --merge_lora true
```

The Python API mirrors it: `swift.llm` exposes the same argument objects, so a script can construct `TrainArguments` and call the trainer factory directly.

Honest limits: the argument surface is huge and moves fast between minor versions, so blog posts and tutorials age quickly; Megatron and RLHF paths assume multi-GPU comfort; and the mixin composition, while elegant, means a debugger steps through more classes than a monolithic trainer would. The payoff is breadth with visible seams - almost everything is one registry entry away from view.
